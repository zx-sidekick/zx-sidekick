## What's in it

The first release. It has one game's definition, Starquake (Bubble Bus Software, 1985); `AUTHORS-GUIDE.md`, in each archive, says how to write one for another game.

- **What you need:** your own copy of the game's tape. For Starquake, that's `Starquake.tap.zip` from World of Spectrum (<https://worldofspectrum.net/item/0004873>). On the first run the program asks for it and checks it's the supported dump; a different one is refused, with a message naming the one it needs. A tape no definition knows still plays, without the panel, training switches or kept scores.
- **Keyboard and pad:** the arrow keys move, Space fires and Enter pauses. Any controller your system knows works, plugged in at any time, with the hints on screen in its maker's letters or symbols. Esc, or Select, opens the guidance menu, which can also turn on the Spectrum's own keyboard for the session.
- **How to play:** a card when a game starts gives the goal and the main moves with their keys and buttons, two of them shown in small copies of your own game playing in a loop.
- **Guidance:** a panel beside the picture, at a level from 0 (off) to 6, each adding to the one below: the teleport codes, the doors' cards and the core's pieces; a map of the rooms walked; the items in them; the items in rooms not reached; routes to the nearest missing piece and to the core; and the whole planet. Up to level 3 it shows only what you could have written down yourself. Hints show while they apply, such as low energy or a door wanting its cards.
- **Training switches:** Full energy, Full bridging platforms, Full laser, Endless lives and No harm from enemies.
- **Saves:** F5 saves and F9 goes back to the last save, at any moment. Quitting during a game saves it, and the next start offers to continue.
- **High scores:** the game's table is kept between runs, each entry marked with the guidance level its game used; a training game's score is shown, then left out. On a pad, a card offers the names entered before, or spells a new one, and another, at a teleport booth, offers the codes you've seen.

`PLAYER-GUIDE.md` has the details: every key and button, the menu, and where the program keeps its files.
